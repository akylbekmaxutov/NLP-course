Theory · The problem

Before attention: one vector had to carry everything

Attention was not invented for Transformers. It was invented in 2014 to fix a specific, visible failure in machine translation — and the failure is worth seeing before the fix.

The machine-translation system of the time was an encoder–decoder RNN. One recurrent network reads the source sentence word by word; its final hidden state is handed to a second recurrent network, which writes the translation word by word. Take the sentence we will use throughout this section:

Source · English

The cat sat on the mat

Target · Russian

Кошка сидела на коврике

The 2014 encoder–decoder. Six words go in, one vector comes out, and the decoder writes the whole translation from that vector alone.

What goes wrong, in order of severity
a fixed budget for a variable job
The context vector c has the same size for a four-word sentence and a forty-word one. The longer the sentence, the more meaning has to be squeezed into the same number of coordinates
the decoder cannot ask again
When it is about to write коврике, the useful evidence is the word mat. That word was read six steps ago and has since been overwritten five times. There is no way to go back and look
distance costs
Lecture 5 measured this: the gradient shrank ×0.48 per step. Information about The has to survive five rewrites to reach c
no parallelism
Step t cannot start until step t−1 finishes. Sentence length is also computation depth

The empirical symptom: translation quality of these models fell off sharply as sentences got longer. Short sentences were fine; long ones degraded, and they degraded in a characteristic way — the beginning of the source was forgotten.

The idea that fixes it. Instead of forcing the decoder to rely on one vector, keep all the encoder states h1…h6 and let the decoder look back at whichever ones are relevant for the word it is writing right now.

Bahdanau’s fix, 2014. Writing коврике, the decoder builds its own context vector by weighting the encoder states — and most of the weight lands on mat.

The word “attention” is descriptive, not metaphorical. Those six numbers 0.03 … 0.71 are real quantities inside the model, they sum to 1, and they are computed fresh for every output word. The rest of this lecture is about where those numbers come from and what happens when you build an entire architecture out of nothing else.

Theory · The core idea

The intuition: relevance, then a weighted average

For the current token, work out which other tokens are relevant, and combine information from them in proportion to that relevance. That is the entire mechanism. Everything else in this lecture is bookkeeping around it.

Attention lets each token build a new version of itself by taking a weighted average of all the tokens in the sequence. The weights say how relevant each other token is to this one, they sum to 1, and they are computed from the data rather than fixed in advance.

The example we will keep coming back to

English

The animal didn’t cross the street because it was tired.

A human reader resolves it without effort: it is the animal. Nothing in the surface form says so. The pronoun sits eight words after its referent, and if you change one word — because it was too wide — the answer flips to street. To build a useful representation of it, the model must reach back and pull in the meaning of animal.

What attention does to one token. The weights are the mechanism; the new vector for it is the product.

The seven questions, answered for attention
What problem does it solve?
Words that depend on each other can be arbitrarily far apart, and a recurrent state cannot hold everything on the way
What is the intuition?
Let each token ask the sequence a question and read back a blend of the answers, weighted by how well each token matches the question
What are the inputs?
One vector per token — the output of the previous layer, or the embeddings if this is the first layer
What operation happens?
Score every pair, scale, softmax, then average the value vectors with those weights
What is the output?
One vector per token again, the same shape as the input — but now context-dependent
Why is it useful?
Any two tokens are one operation apart regardless of distance, and every position is computed in parallel
Where does it appear?
Three places in a Transformer: encoder self-attention, decoder masked self-attention, and decoder cross-attention

Keep this list. Every component introduced from here on — the FFN, the residual, LayerNorm, positional encoding — will be introduced by answering the same seven questions.

Theory · The three roles

Query, Key and Value

“How relevant is token j to token i” is not a single question, so a single vector per token cannot answer it. Attention gives every token three vectors instead of one.

Query · what I am looking for

The token’s question. For it, something like “which noun could I be standing in for?”

Key · what I advertise

The token’s label, as seen by everyone else. For animal, something like “I am a singular animate noun.”

Value · what I hand over

The content that actually gets copied if this token is selected. For animal, its meaning — which need not look anything like its key.

Why key and value differ

Being findable and being useful are different jobs. Tying them would force one vector to do both, and the model would lose the freedom to advertise one thing and carry another.

The database analogy, and its limit. In a dictionary you give a key, find the one matching entry, and get its value. Attention does the same thing softly: your query is compared with every key, and you get back a blend of all the values, weighted by match quality. There is no winner and no lookup failure — every token always contributes something, however little.

Where the three vectors come from

They are three linear projections of the same input. Nothing more mysterious than three matrix multiplications: 

Q = X W_Q        K = X W_K        V = X W_V

Q, K and V are three views of the same tokens. The matrices start random and are shaped entirely by training.

Every symbol in Q = X W_Q
X
the input matrix, shape (n, d_model): one row per token, each row the vector arriving from the previous layer. In layer 1 these are embeddings + positions
W_Q, W_K, W_V
three learned matrices of shape (d_model, d_model) in the standard setup. They are parameters — random at initialisation, adjusted by gradient descent, shared across all positions and all sequences
Q
(n, d_model). Row i is what token i is looking for
K
(n, d_model). Row j is what token j advertises
V
(n, d_model). Row j is what token j contributes when selected

Note what is shared and what is not. Every token uses the same three matrices — there is no per-position parameter anywhere in attention. Different tokens get different Q, K and V only because their input vectors differ. That is also why attention works on sequences of any length.

Softmax turns a row of arbitrary real numbers into non-negative weights that sum to 1: softmax(s)ₖ = e^{sₖ} / Σ₃ e^{s₃}. Applied along each row separately, so every token distributes exactly one unit of attention.

The row for жақсы is the point of the whole example. It spends 0.576 of its attention on емес, 0.284 on қосымша and only 0.140 on itself. A raw score gap of 2 versus 1 has become a weight ratio of about 2:1 — softmax sharpens differences without eliminating the smaller ones.

Step 5 · Multiply by V

The last step is the one students most often mis-picture. Attention does not select a token. It builds a new vector as a weighted sum of value vectors: 

new vector for «жақсы»
    = 0.284 · v(қосымша) + 0.140 · v(жақсы) + 0.576 · v(емес)
    = 0.284 · [1, 1]     + 0.140 · [0, 1]   + 0.576 · [2, 3]
    = [1.436, 2.152]

Not a selection, a blend. Every value contributes; the weights decide how much.

Theory · One detail that is not a detail

Why divide by √dₖ

“For numerical stability” is the answer people repeat and it explains nothing. Here is the actual argument, and then the measurement.

The argument in three steps
1. dot products grow with dimension
If q and k have dₖ independent components with mean 0 and variance 1, their dot product has mean 0 and variance dₖ. Its typical size is therefore √dₖ. At dₖ = 64 that is 8× larger than at dₖ = 1, purely from the dimension
2. softmax is scale-sensitive
Softmax cares about differences between scores. Multiply all scores by 8 and a modest gap of 2 becomes a gap of 16, and e¹⁶ is nearly nine million times e⁰. One token takes essentially all the weight
3. a saturated softmax has no gradient
Where the output is 1.000 and 0.000, the derivative of softmax is almost exactly zero. No gradient reaches W_Q or W_K, and the layer stops learning — permanently, since it cannot move away from where it is stuck

The fix: divide the scores by √dₖ, which cancels exactly the growth introduced by the dimension and returns the scores to a range where softmax still has slope.

The same four scores, before and after dividing by √d. Scaling does not change which token wins — it changes by how much.

Theory · Putting it together

The complete formula, as four operations

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax( Q·Kᵀ / √dₖ ) · V

Read left to right, it is exactly the five steps you just computed by hand, with steps 4 and 5 written as one product. Learn it as four verbs, not as a string of symbols.

The four steps. Nothing here is a loop: every pair of positions is scored in one matrix multiplication.

Every symbol, with its interpretation
Q·Kᵀ
compare. A matrix of shape (n, n): cell (i, j) is how well token i’s question matches token j’s advertisement. High when the two vectors point the same way — which is what “relevant” means operationally
√dₖ
scale. dₖ is the width of one query vector. Dividing by its square root removes the growth that dimension alone would cause
softmax(·)
normalise. Applied along the last axis, so each row becomes a probability distribution over tokens. Row i answers “where should token i get its information from?”
· V
gather. The weights multiply the value matrix, producing one new vector per token — each a weighted blend of every value
the output
shape (n, dₖ). Same number of tokens as went in; each one now carries information from the tokens it found relevant

If you remember one sentence: queries and keys decide where to look, values decide what is read, and softmax makes the looking add up to one.

print("K  what do I offer         ", tuple(K.shape))

# 2. scale, so the numbers stay in a range softmax can work with
scores = scores / math.sqrt(Q.size(-1))

Theory · The variant that built the Transformer

Self-attention

In Bahdanau’s translation model the queries came from the decoder and the keys came from the encoder — two different sequences. Self-attention is what you get when you point the mechanism at a single sequence.

Self-attention is attention where Q, K and V are all computed from the same sequence. Every token queries the very sequence it belongs to, itself included. Hence “self”.

English

The scientist published her paper because she finished the experiments.

Three different tokens in this sentence need three different things. her needs to know whose paper it is. she needs its referent. finished needs its object. One self-attention layer computes all three at the same time, because every row of the weight matrix is an independent decision.

One layer, three independent decisions. Each row of the attention matrix is computed from its own query and does not constrain the others.

What a token means before

The embedding of her is the same vector in every sentence in the corpus. It encodes the word, not this use of it.

What it means after

The output vector for her is built mostly from the value of scientist. Two sentences with different referents give two different vectors for the same word.

This is the single most important consequence of the whole lecture. After self-attention, a token’s representation is no longer a property of the token — it is a property of the token in this sequence. Lecture 4’s word2vec gave банк one vector forever; self-attention gives it one vector per sentence. That is the difference between a static embedding and a contextual one, and it is why Lecture 7 can build BERT out of exactly this.

Theory · The family

The kinds of attention, and how they differ

Two things vary between attention variants: how the score is computed, and where Q, K and V come from. Nothing else. Here are both axes.

Axis 1 · How the score is computed

Three scoring functions. The Transformer picked the cheapest one and fixed its one weakness with a division.

Why the Transformer chose scaled dot-product
additive attention
score(q, k) = vᵀ tanh(Wₕq + Wₖk). A one-hidden-layer network evaluated for every query–key pair. It works well and it handles queries and keys of different widths, but it cannot be written as a single matrix product, so on modern hardware it is far slower
dot-product attention
score(q, k) = q·k. No parameters at all, and the entire n×n score matrix is one matrix multiplication — the operation GPUs are built for. Its weakness is exactly the one we measured: at large d the scores blow up
scaled dot-product
score(q, k) = q·k / √dₖ. Keeps the speed, removes the weakness. The 2017 paper compared the two directly and found them equally accurate at small d, with additive attention ahead at large d — until the scaling was added

The lesson is not “dot products are better”. It is that an operation which maps onto one matrix multiplication can be scaled to billions of parameters, and one that cannot, cannot.

Theory · Masking

Causal masking: hiding the future

A model trained to predict the next token must never be shown that token. With attention, “never shown” has a precise implementation.

Take I love deep learning. During training we want all four next-token predictions computed in one pass — that parallelism is the whole reason to use a Transformer. But position 2 must predict deep while being able to see only I love. Each position therefore gets its own allowed window:

The causal mask. Row t has exactly t+1 blue cells — a good invariant to assert in your own code.

The mask, question by question
What problem does it solve?
Without it, predicting the next token is trivial: the token is right there in the input, and the model learns to copy it. Training loss falls to zero and the model generates nothing useful
Why −inf rather than 0?
Setting the score to zero would still leave weight e⁰ = 1 after softmax. Setting it to −inf gives e^(−inf) = 0, an exact zero, and no gradient path
Why is training still parallel?
All n predictions are computed in one forward pass; the mask is what keeps them honest. This is the key efficiency difference from an RNN, which really must run n steps
Does generation use it too?
Yes, though at generation time there is no future to hide — the tokens simply do not exist yet. The mask matters most during training

Padding masks and causal masks are the same machinery. Both write −inf into the score matrix before the softmax. They differ only in which cells they choose, and a batched decoder applies both at once.

Theory · More than one question at a time

Multi-head attention

One attention operation produces one set of weights per token, so it can follow one kind of relationship. A sentence has several running at once.

English

The bank approved the loan because it considered the applicant reliable.

To build a good vector for it, several different relationships matter simultaneously, and each has a different answer:

Coreference

it → bank. Which noun am I standing for?

Subject–verb

it → considered. Which verb am I the subject of?

Local syntax

it → because. What clause am I inside?

Topic

it → loan. What is this sentence about?

A single softmax must compromise between these — it has one unit of weight to distribute and four things to spend it on. The fix is to run several attention operations in parallel, each in its own projection space, and concatenate what they find.

Eight heads of 64 dimensions, not one head of 512. The final projection Wₒ is what lets a token combine what different heads found.

Heads are not assigned roles. Nobody tells head 3 to track coreference. Every head starts from random projections and is shaped only by the gradient of the training loss. When researchers later inspect a trained model and report “this head follows syntactic dependencies”, they are describing something that emerged. Many heads turn out to do nothing identifiable at all, and a well-known result is that a large fraction of them can be pruned after training with little loss.

The dimensions, worked out

The original paper used d_model = 512 and h = 8 heads, giving dₖ = dₕ = 512 / 8 = 64. That division is the central trick: the heads split the existing width rather than adding to it.

Architecture · The turn

From attention to the Transformer

Until 2017 attention was an addition to a recurrent network: the RNN did the sequence modelling, attention helped the decoder look back. Then someone asked the obvious question.

What if we remove the recurrence entirely and build the whole sequence model out of attention? The paper that did this was called Attention Is All You Need, and the title is the argument.

Recurrence compared with attention

The trade is explicit: attention gives up cheap scaling in sequence length and built-in ordering, and buys a constant-length path between any two tokens plus complete parallelism. On the hardware of 2017 — and much more so on the hardware of today — that trade was overwhelmingly worth making. The two costs are fixable: word order is restored by adding a positional signal, and the quadratic term is tolerable at the sequence lengths people actually use.

The whole architecture, before we take it apart

Look at this once as a picture. Every box in it is explained in the sections that follow, and we will come back to this diagram each time.

The 2017 architecture in full. Three attention layers, in three different roles: encoder self, decoder masked self, and cross.

What to notice on first sight
there is no recurrence anywhere
No arrow goes from one token to the next. Every arrow goes from one layer to the next, and inside a layer all tokens are processed together
attention appears three times, not once
Encoder self-attention (source reads source), decoder masked self-attention (target reads target, past only), and cross-attention (target reads source). Same formula, three different sources of Q, K and V

Six times in this picture. These are the residual connections and layer normalisation — unglamorous, and the reason the thing trains at all
only one arrow crosses

The rest of this lecture walks up this diagram — input, encoder block, stacking, decoder block, cross-attention, output layer — and then rebuilds it three different ways as BERT, GPT and T5.

Architecture · The bottom of the diagram

From a string to a matrix

A Transformer consumes a matrix of floats. Getting a sentence into that form takes three steps, and each one is a design decision.

Four steps from a string to one row of the input matrix X. Repeat for every token and you have (n, 512).

The three steps, and what each decides
tokenization
Lecture 2 covered this. Modern Transformers use sub-word tokenizers (BPE, WordPiece, SentencePiece) rather than whole words, so there is no out-of-vocabulary problem — an unseen word is spelled out of familiar pieces. This matters enormously for Kazakh: кітаптарымыздан need not be its own vocabulary entry, it can be assembled from a root and suffixes
token ids
Each token becomes an integer index. The ids carry no meaning — id 8106 is not “more” than id 412. They are addresses, nothing else
embedding lookup
The embedding table is a learned matrix of shape (vocab, d_model). Looking up an id is selecting a row. This is the same object as Lecture 4’s word2vec table, except it is trained jointly with the rest of the model instead of separately
positional information
Added, not concatenated. The next section explains why it is needed at all

Where the parameters are: at 30 000 × 512 the embedding table alone holds 15.4 million parameters — more than any single Transformer block. In small models the embedding table is usually the largest thing in the file.

Architecture · The missing piece

Attention cannot tell word order

This is the flaw in everything above, and it is worth pausing on, because the whole point of Lecture 5 was that order matters.

Two sentences, one bag of words

dog bites man

man bites dog

Look again at softmax(Q·Kᵀ/√d)·V. No term in it refers to a position. Permute the rows of X and every row of the output is permuted the same way — the mechanism is equivariant to reordering. To a bare self-attention layer, those two sentences are the same input in a different order, and the representation it builds for dog is identical in both. Without a fix, a Transformer is a very expensive bag of words.

Sinusoidal positional encoding

The original solution gives each position a fixed vector built from sines and cosines at many frequencies: 

PE(pos, 2i)   = sin( pos / 10000^(2i/d) )
PE(pos, 2i+1) = cos( pos / 10000^(2i/d) )

    pos = which position in the sequence
    i   = which pair of dimensions
    d   = d_model

Like the digits of an odometer: the last digit changes every step, the first almost never. No two positions get the same combination.

Architecture · The building block

One encoder block, component by component

Six pieces, in this order: multi-head self-attention, a residual, layer normalisation, a feed-forward network, another residual, another layer normalisation. Each one exists for a reason you can state in a sentence.

Attention moves information sideways between tokens; the feed-forward layer works on each token independently. Both are wrapped in a residual, so the block adds to its input rather than replacing it.

1 · Multi-head self-attention

What it is for: this is the only component in the entire Transformer where information moves between positions. Delete it and you are left with a model that processes each token in isolation — no context, no relationships, no language. Every token reads every token and updates itself with what it found.

2 · The residual connection

x  ←  x + Attention(x)

The sub-layer’s output is added to its input rather than replacing it. Two consequences, both essential:

A residual turns a stack of replacements into a stack of corrections. The original vector always has an unobstructed route to the output — and the gradient has one back.

What the addition buys
gradient flow
The derivative of x + f(x) with respect to x is 1 + f′(x). That 1 is a direct path: however small f′ becomes, the gradient still arrives. Without it, a 24-layer stack multiplies 24 small numbers together and the early layers never learn
information preservation
The block can only adjust the representation. If attention finds nothing useful for a token, outputting near-zero leaves the token as it was — a safe default. Without the residual, “do nothing” would have to be learned
the same trick you already know
Lecture 5’s LSTM cell state was added to rather than rewritten, for exactly this reason. Residual connections are that idea applied to depth instead of time

Measured on our own model: input norm 27.74, attention output alone 5.65, after adding the residual 28.77. The block adjusts by about one part in five; it does not overwrite.

3 · Layer normalisation

LayerNorm takes one token’s vector, subtracts its mean and divides by its standard deviation, then rescales with two learned parameters. It normalises across the features of one token — never across the batch, and never across the sequence. 

LayerNorm(x) = γ · (x − mean(x)) / sqrt(var(x) + ε) + β

    mean, var  computed over the d_model features of THIS token
    γ, β       learned, one per feature
Why normalise at all
the problem
Every sub-layer changes the scale of its output a little. Stack 24 of them and the vectors arriving at the last layer may be a hundred times larger or smaller than what the layer’s weights were tuned for. Training becomes a moving target
the fix
Reset each token’s vector to mean 0 and variance 1 before the sub-layer reads it. Every layer then sees inputs on a predictable scale, whatever the layers below did
why not BatchNorm
BatchNorm normalises across examples in the batch, which makes each token’s representation depend on the other sentences it happened to be batched with, and breaks entirely at generation time with a batch of one. LayerNorm involves only the token itself
γ and β
Normalising throws away scale and offset, which are sometimes useful. The two learned vectors let the model put back exactly as much as it wants

Pre-norm or post-norm? The 2017 paper normalised after the residual addition. Practice moved to normalising before the sub-layer, as in the diagram above, because it keeps the residual path completely clean and lets deep models train without a warm-up schedule. Almost every model since 2020 is pre-norm.

4 · The feed-forward network

FFN(x) = W₂ · σ(W₁x + b₁) + b₂

    W₁: (512, 2048)   expand
    σ  : ReLU in 2017, GELU or SwiGLU today
    W₂: (2048, 512)   project back

It is an ordinary two-layer MLP, applied to every position independently and with the same weights. Token 3 and token 40 go through the identical network and never meet inside it.

Attention mixes across tokens, the FFN transforms within one

If you take away one structural idea from this lecture, take this one. The two halves of a Transformer block move in perpendicular directions.

Two orthogonal operations. Attention is the only one that crosses between columns; the FFN never does.

Architecture · Depth

Stacking blocks: what actually changes with depth

“Stack N of them” is a true but empty instruction. The question worth answering is what is different about the representation at layer 12 compared with layer 1.

What deeper layers appear to represent. Treat the labels as a tendency observed in probing experiments, not as a specification anyone programmed.

What depth actually does
context widens
After one block a token has seen every other token — but only their pre-context versions. After two blocks it sees tokens that have themselves already seen everything. Information composes: layer 3 can use a relationship that only becomes visible once layer 2 has resolved something else
the representation drifts from the word
Early layers stay close to the embedding, so probes recover the identity of the word easily. Deep layers encode what the word is doing here, and the word itself becomes harder to recover
the shape never changes
(batch, tokens, 512) in, (batch, tokens, 512) out, for every block. That invariant is the entire reason stacking is possible, and why you can take 12 layers of a trained model and use only the first 6
each block has its own weights
Blocks are not shared. 12 blocks means 12 independent copies of the parameters — this is where a large model’s size comes from

Typical depths: the 2017 paper used 6 encoder and 6 decoder blocks; BERT-base 12; BERT-large 24; GPT-3 96. Depth, not width, is usually the first thing scaled.

Architecture · The other stack

The decoder block

The decoder produces the output sequence, one token at a time. Its block has the encoder’s two sub-layers plus a third, and the third is the interesting one.

A decoder block. Sub-layer 1 looks at what has been written; sub-layer 2 looks at what is being translated; sub-layer 3 thinks about it.

Masked self-attention asks: what have I written so far?

Q, K and V all come from the partial output. Writing жақсы in Мен машиналық оқытуды жақсы…, the model needs to know it has already produced the object of the sentence. Masked, because it must not see the words it has yet to write.

Cross-attention asks: what in the source is relevant now?

Q comes from the target, K and V from the encoder output. The token being written queries the source sentence, and the source hands back the part that matters — for оқытуды, the English learning.

Why both are needed, in one sentence each. Without masked self-attention the decoder would not know what it has already said, and would repeat itself or lose agreement. Without cross-attention it would not know what it is translating, and would produce fluent text unrelated to the source.

Architecture · The bridge

Cross-attention in detail

Cross-attention is the same four steps with one change: the queries and the keys come from different sequences. That single change is what connects two languages. 

self-attention    Q = X W_Q      K = X W_K       V = X W_V
                  (target)       (target)        (target)

cross-attention   Q = Y W_Q      K = H W_K       V = H W_V
                  (target)       (encoder out)   (encoder out)

A cross-attention map is a soft alignment between two languages — learned from parallel text alone, with no dictionary supplied.

Cross-attention, question by question
What are the inputs?
Two sequences: the decoder’s current representations Y of shape (m, d), and the encoder’s output H of shape (n, d). They need not have the same length
What is the score matrix?
Shape (m, n) — rectangular, not square. Row i is target token i’s distribution over the source
Is it masked?
Not causally. The whole source is available from the start; there is nothing to hide. Only the source’s padding is masked
What is the output?
Shape (m, d) — one vector per target token, containing what that token pulled from the source
Where does it appear?
Only in encoder–decoder models: the original Transformer, T5, BART, Whisper. Decoder-only models such as GPT have no cross-attention at all, because they have no separate encoder

The historical circle closes here. This is precisely Bahdanau’s 2014 idea from the start of the lecture — the decoder looking back at all encoder states — expressed in the Q/K/V form and computed with a matrix multiplication.

Three things the diagram makes visible

Architecture · The last layer

From a hidden vector to the next token

The stack ends with one vector per position. Turning that into a word takes exactly two operations, and they are the same two you have already seen.

Causal language modelling

The capital of Kazakhstan is ___

Illustrative probabilities. The mechanism is what matters: one linear layer per position, then a softmax over the whole vocabulary.

The output head, in detail
the linear layer
Shape (d_model, vocab). Column w is a learned vector for word w, and the logit for w is the dot product of the hidden state with that column — the same similarity operation as attention scores, over the vocabulary instead of over the sequence
weight tying
Most models reuse the input embedding matrix, transposed, as this output matrix. It saves vocab × d_model parameters — 15 million in our example — and usually improves quality
softmax
The same function as inside attention, applied over the vocabulary axis instead of the sequence axis. Turns 30 000 arbitrary scores into 30 000 probabilities that sum to 1
choosing a token
Greedy takes the argmax; sampling draws from the distribution; temperature sharpens or flattens it before drawing; top-k and top-p truncate the tail first. This choice is decoding strategy, not architecture — Lecture 8 covers it
during training
No token is chosen at all. The cross-entropy loss reads off the probability assigned to the correct next token and pushes it up. Every position in every sequence contributes one such term

This is the entire objective of a language model. Predict the next token, at every position, over and over, on enormous amounts of text. Everything a large model appears to know is a side effect of getting good at that one task.

Architecture · Three descendants

Encoder-only, decoder-only, encoder–decoder

The 2017 paper described one architecture. Almost everything since is one of its two halves, or both. The choice follows from a single question: does the model need to generate?

Same block, three wirings. The difference is the mask and whether a second stack exists.

Encoder-only — BERT and its family

What it does

Reads the whole input with bidirectional attention and emits one contextual vector per token. It cannot generate text, and does not try.

What it is for

Sequence classification (sentiment, intent), token classification (NER, part of speech), sentence embeddings, retrieval, reranking, extractive question answering.

Why bidirectional helps

To label банк in Ол банкте отыр, the words after it are as informative as the words before. A classifier has no reason to give up half the evidence.

How it is trained

Not by next-token prediction — that would be trivial when you can see the future. Instead words are masked out and reconstructed. Lecture 7 is entirely about this.

Decoder-only — GPT, Llama, Qwen, Gemma

One stack with causal masking, trained to predict the next token. This is what essentially every modern large language model is, and the reasons it won are worth stating explicitly:

Why decoder-only became dominant
the training signal is free and enormous
Any text is training data, and every token is a supervised example. A 1 000-token document yields 1 000 predictions. No labels, no parallel corpus, no annotation
one interface for every task
Translation, summarisation, classification and question answering all become “continue this text”. An encoder–decoder needs a separate input and output convention per task; a decoder needs a prompt
simplicity scales
One stack, one mask, one objective. Fewer moving parts is a real advantage when you are training for months on thousands of accelerators
generation is native
The model is trained in exactly the mode it is used in. There is no mismatch between pretraining and deployment

The cost is real: a causal model builds worse representations of the input than a bidirectional one, because it never looks right. For pure classification on a fixed dataset, a BERT-size encoder is often still the better and much cheaper tool.

Encoder–decoder — T5, BART, the original

Both stacks, joined by cross-attention. The natural fit when the input and the output are two clearly separate sequences: translation, speech recognition, and anything where the source should be encoded bidirectionally while the target is generated left to right. T5 pushed this furthest by phrasing every task as text-to-text.

Architecture · What changed since 2017

How modern LLM Transformers differ

The skeleton you have just learned is still exactly right. Modern models replace several components with better versions — each a local substitution, none of them changing the shape of the story.

Five substitutions, one skeleton. Everything in blue at the bottom is what you have already learned, and it did not change.

Each change, and what it buys
pre-norm instead of post-norm
Normalise before the sub-layer, not after the residual addition. Keeps the residual path free of any normalisation, which lets very deep stacks train without a learning-rate warm-up. Essentially universal since GPT-2
RMSNorm instead of LayerNorm
Divide by the root-mean-square of the vector and skip the mean subtraction and the bias. Fewer operations, no measurable quality loss. A pure efficiency win, used by Llama, Qwen, Gemma and Mistral
GELU and SwiGLU instead of ReLU
GELU is a smooth ReLU. SwiGLU splits the expansion into two branches, one acting as a learned gate on the other: FFN(x) = (Swish(xW₁) ⊙ xW₃)W₂. It uses three matrices instead of two, so the hidden width is usually cut to about 2/3 to keep the parameter count level. Consistently a little better
RoPE instead of an additive position vector
Rotary Position Embedding rotates each query and key by an angle proportional to its position, inside the attention computation rather than at the input. The dot product between a rotated query and a rotated key then depends only on their relative distance — which is what attention actually needs, and what the sinusoidal table only approximated. It also extends to longer contexts far more gracefully
MQA and GQA instead of full multi-head
Keep all h query heads, but share keys and values across them: one shared set (multi-query) or one per group of heads (grouped-query). Quality is nearly unchanged and the KV cache shrinks by the sharing factor — which is the binding constraint when serving long conversations
KV cache
Not an architecture change but the central inference trick. When generating token t+1, the keys and values of tokens 1…t are identical to what they were a step ago, so they are stored and reused. Generation goes from re-reading the whole prefix each step to appending one row. The cost moves from compute to memory, which is why GQA matters so much

None of this is on the exam, and all of it is optional. If you open the source of Llama or Qwen after this lecture, you will recognise the block structure immediately, and these six names are the vocabulary for the parts that look unfamiliar.

What has not changed since 2017. Queries, keys and values. The dot product as a similarity. The √d scaling. Softmax over the sequence. Multiple heads. Residual connections. A per-token feed-forward network. Stacked identical blocks. Next-token prediction. Nine years of intense work, and the core of the mechanism is exactly what you computed by hand earlier on this page.

1 · Scaled dot-product attention

import math

import torch


def attention(query, key, value, mask=None):
    """query, key, value: (batch, heads, tokens, d_k)"""
    scores = query @ key.transpose(-2, -1)          # (batch, heads, tokens, tokens)
    scores = scores / math.sqrt(query.size(-1))
    if mask is not None:
        scores = scores.masked_fill(mask, float("-inf"))
    weights = scores.softmax(dim=-1)
    return weights @ value, weights                 # (batch, heads, tokens, d_k)
Line by line, against the formula
query @ key.transpose(-2, -1)
Q·Kᵀ — every query meets every key. transpose(-2, -1) swaps the last two axes only, leaving batch and head axes alone, so this is one batched matrix multiplication over all examples and all heads at once
/ math.sqrt(query.size(-1))
÷ √dₖ — size(-1) is the per-head width, 64 in our running example, not d_model. Getting this wrong is a common and quiet bug
masked_fill(mask, -inf)
The causal or padding mask, applied before the softmax so the forbidden weights become exactly zero
scores.softmax(dim=-1)
softmax along the last axis, which is the key axis. Each row now sums to 1. Using dim=-2 here would normalise the wrong way and produce a model that trains but never works well
weights @ value
· V — the weighted average of the value vectors. The result has the same shape as query

In production you would call F.scaled_dot_product_attention(q, k, v, is_causal=True), which runs a fused kernel that never materialises the full (tokens, tokens) matrix in memory. It computes exactly these five lines.

2 · Multi-head attention

import torch.nn as nn


class MultiHeadAttention(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, dim, heads):
        super().__init__()
        assert dim % heads == 0
        self.heads, self.size = heads, dim // heads
        self.query = nn.Linear(dim, dim)            # W_Q
        self.key = nn.Linear(dim, dim)              # W_K
        self.value = nn.Linear(dim, dim)            # W_V
        self.out = nn.Linear(dim, dim)              # W_O

    def split(self, x):                             # (b, t, dim) -> (b, heads, t, size)
        batch, tokens, _ = x.shape
        return x.view(batch, tokens, self.heads, self.size).transpose(1, 2)

    def forward(self, x, mask=None):
        q = self.split(self.query(x))
        k = self.split(self.key(x))
        v = self.split(self.value(x))

        out, weights = attention(q, k, v, mask)     # (b, heads, t, size)

        batch, _, tokens, _ = out.shape
        out = out.transpose(1, 2).reshape(batch, tokens, self.heads * self.size)
        return self.out(out), weights               # (b, t, dim)
The five blocks of this class
Q/K/V projections
Three nn.Linear(dim, dim) layers. Note that all heads are computed by one linear layer each, not h separate ones — the split into heads happens afterwards, which is why multiple heads cost nothing extra
reshape into heads
view reads the dim numbers as (heads, size); transpose(1, 2) moves the head axis in front of the token axis so that the matrix multiplications inside attention operate per head
attention
The function above, unchanged. Everything batched: (batch × heads) independent attention maps computed in one call
concatenate heads
transpose(1, 2).reshape(...) is the exact inverse of the split. Concatenation is a reshape — no copying of meaning, just a different view of the same numbers
output projection
self.out is Wₒ. Without it the head outputs would sit in fixed, non-interacting slices of the vector; this layer is what lets a token combine what different heads found

The one subtlety: reshape rather than view after the transpose, because the transposed tensor is no longer contiguous in memory. This is the single most common runtime error when people write this class from memory.

3 · A Transformer block

class Block(nn.Module):
    """pre-norm: LayerNorm -> sub-layer -> add"""

    def __init__(self, dim, heads, hidden, dropout=0.1):
        super().__init__()
        self.attention = MultiHeadAttention(dim, heads)
        self.norm1 = nn.LayerNorm(dim)
        self.norm2 = nn.LayerNorm(dim)
        self.feed_forward = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(dim, hidden),                 # W_1: expand
            nn.GELU(),                              # the non-linearity
            nn.Linear(hidden, dim),                 # W_2: project back
        )
        self.dropout = nn.Dropout(dropout)

    def forward(self, x, mask=None):
        attended, weights = self.attention(self.norm1(x), mask)
        x = x + self.dropout(attended)                            # residual 1
        x = x + self.dropout(self.feed_forward(self.norm2(x)))    # residual 2
        return x, weights

The block diagram and the forward method are the same object drawn two ways.

Two details worth arguing about in class. First, this is pre-norm: norm1 is applied to the input of the sub-layer, and the residual adds the raw x. The 2017 paper wrote x = norm(x + attention(x)) instead. Second, hidden is conventionally 4 × dim, which is where two thirds of the block’s parameters come from.

4 · Assembling the model

class Transformer(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, vocab_size, classes, dim=128, heads=4, layers=2,
                 hidden=256, max_len=60, dropout=0.1):
        super().__init__()
        self.embedding = nn.Embedding(vocab_size, dim, padding_idx=PAD)
        self.register_buffer("positions", positional_encoding(max_len, dim))
        self.blocks = nn.ModuleList(
            [Block(dim, heads, hidden, dropout) for _ in range(layers)])
        self.norm = nn.LayerNorm(dim)
        self.out = nn.Linear(dim, classes)

    def forward(self, ids, pad_mask):
        x = self.embedding(ids) + self.positions[:ids.size(1)]     # (b, t, dim)
        mask = pad_mask[:, None, None, :]                          # (b, 1, 1, t)
        for block in self.blocks:
            x, weights = block(x, mask)
        x = self.norm(x)

        # mean over the real tokens only — padding must not dilute the average
        keep = (~pad_mask).unsqueeze(-1).float()
        pooled = (x * keep).sum(1) / keep.sum(1).clamp(min=1)       # (b, dim)
        return self.out(pooled)                                     # (b, classes)
Reading the assembly
embedding(ids) + positions
The two input steps from the “string to matrix” section, on one line. register_buffer stores the sinusoidal table with the model but marks it as not trainable
pad_mask[:, None, None, :]
Shape (b, t) becomes (b, 1, 1, t) so it broadcasts across heads and across query positions. The mask names columns — keys that may not be read
the block loop
Each block returns the same shape it received, which is what makes this a plain loop with no bookkeeping
masked mean pooling
Classification needs one vector per sequence, not per token. Multiplying by keep zeroes the padding rows and dividing by keep.sum(1) divides by the real length. BERT instead prepends [CLS] and reads its row — either is fine
self.out
For a language model this would be nn.Linear(dim, vocab_size) applied at every position. For a classifier it is dim → classes applied once. Same idea, different target set

To turn this into a language model, change three things: add a causal mask, project to vocab_size at every position instead of pooling, and train with cross-entropy against the input shifted by one. The blocks do not change at all.

Summary · The test of understanding

One token, all the way through

If you can narrate this journey without looking, you have the lecture. Take the token емес in қосымша жақсы емес, in a decoder-only model with d_model = 512.

Eleven steps, no recurrence. The vector keeps its shape from the embedding lookup to the output head; what changes is how much of the rest of the sentence is baked into it.

The fourteen questions this lecture must answer
Where does Q come from?
The token’s own input vector times the learned matrix W_Q. In cross-attention, from the target sequence
Where does K come from?
Each token’s input vector times W_K. In cross-attention, from the encoder output
Where does V come from?
The same vectors as K, times W_V. Same source as K, always
What does Q·Kᵀ mean?
A table of relevance: cell (i, j) is how well token i’s question matches token j’s advertisement
Why softmax?
To turn arbitrary real scores into non-negative weights that sum to 1, so the result is an average rather than an unbounded sum — and so that relative differences, not absolute magnitudes, decide the outcome
Why divide by √dₖ?
Dot products grow like √dₖ. Undivided, the softmax saturates to a hard 1/0 choice with a near-zero derivative and the layer stops learning. Measured: entropy 1.879 → 0.359
What does the output vector represent?
The token, rewritten to include what it found relevant elsewhere in the sequence. Same shape as the input, different contents
Why multiple heads?
One softmax can express one relevance pattern. Several heads in separate subspaces can follow coreference, syntax and topic at once — and splitting a fixed width costs nothing
Why is positional information needed?
Attention is permutation-equivariant: we measured that shuffling the input just shuffles the output. Without positions, dog bites man and man bites dog are the same input
What happens inside one block?
Normalise, attend, add the residual; normalise, feed-forward, add the residual. Shape in equals shape out
Attention versus the FFN?
Attention mixes information across tokens with input-dependent weights. The FFN transforms each token independently with fixed weights, and holds two thirds of the parameters
What are residuals doing?
Making every sub-layer a correction rather than a replacement, so the gradient has an unobstructed path through any depth and “change nothing” is the default
What is masking?
Writing −inf into chosen cells of the score matrix before the softmax. Causal masks hide the future; padding masks hide empty slots
What is cross-attention?
Attention with Q from one sequence and K, V from another. It is how a decoder reads an encoder, and how translation aligns two languages
How is the next token predicted?
Take the last position’s vector after the final block, project it to vocabulary size with one linear layer, softmax over the vocabulary, then pick or sample from that distribution

If any of those is still shaky, the section that answers it is linked from the contents on the left. They are deliberately short enough to reread.

In class

Knowledge check

Eight questions we answer together before finishing.

Question 1

What exactly was the bottleneck in an encoder–decoder RNN, and how does attention remove it?

Show Answer

The whole source sentence had to fit into one fixed-size context vector, and the decoder could never look back at the individual encoder states. Attention keeps all of them and lets the decoder compute a fresh weighted combination for every output token.

Question 2

Why is the value vector separate from the key?

Show Answer

Because being easy to find and being useful to read are different jobs. The key decides whether a token is selected; the value is what it contributes once selected. Tying them would force one vector to do both.

Question 3

In the hand-worked example, the row for жақсы was [0.284, 0.140, 0.576]. What do those three numbers mean, and why do they sum to 1?

Show Answer

They are how much of жақсы’s new vector is taken from each token’s value: 28.4% from қосымша, 14.0% from itself, 57.6% from емес. They sum to 1 because softmax normalises each row, so the output is a weighted average rather than an unbounded sum.

Question 4

We divide the scores by √dₖ. What breaks without it?

Show Answer

The softmax saturates. We measured it: at d = 64 the largest weight goes to 1.000 and the entropy collapses from 1.879 to 0.359. A saturated softmax has a near-zero derivative, so the layer stops receiving gradient and cannot recover.

Question 5

Shuffling the words gave an identical attention output. Why is that a problem, and what fixes it?

Show Answer

It means attention is a bag of words — the exact failure Lecture 5 existed to fix. Adding a distinct vector per position fixes it; we measured the outputs diverging by 1.166 once the positional table was added.

Question 6

A decoder block has three sub-layers. What would go wrong if you deleted the cross-attention? And the masked self-attention?

Show Answer

Without cross-attention the decoder cannot see the source at all — it would generate fluent but unrelated text, a language model rather than a translator. Without masked self-attention it would not know what it has already written, losing agreement and repeating itself.

Question 7

Attention gets the name. Where are most of the parameters?

Show Answer

In the feed-forward network. With the standard 4× expansion the FFN has about 2× the parameters of the attention sub-layer, and in a full model the embedding table is larger still. Attention decides what a token sees; the FFN is where most stored knowledge sits.

Question 8

Our Transformer scored 0.750, below TF-IDF’s 0.780. Is the architecture wrong?

Show Answer

No — it is data-starved. More epochs gave 0.749 and a smaller model gave 0.750, so it is neither undertrained nor oversized. Five times the data gave 0.787. Self-attention has to learn relationships from labels alone, and 8 000 short reviews cannot support that.

Self-check quiz

Ten questions with instant feedback.

  1. What does attention actually compute?

    Weights from queries and keys, applied to values. Everything else is bookkeeping.

  2. In Q = X W_Q, what is W_Q?

    There is no per-position parameter in attention. Tokens differ only because their inputs differ.

  3. Cell (i, j) of Q·Kᵀ measures…

    And it is not symmetric: i asking about j is a different question from j asking about i.

  4. Why divide the scores by √dₖ?

    Measured: without it the largest weight is 1.000 and the entropy falls to 0.359 out of 2.303.

  5. Shuffling the input words leaves plain self-attention’s output…

    That is why positional encoding exists. Without it a Transformer is a bag of words.

  6. What does a causal mask do?

    Row t ends up with exactly t+1 non-zero weights. Without it, next-token prediction is trivial.

  7. Eight heads of 64 dimensions, against one head of 512, cost…

    Splitting is a reshape. Multi-head attention buys diversity essentially for free.

  8. In decoder cross-attention, where do K and V come from?

    Q from the target, K and V from the source. That single asymmetry is what links the two sequences.

  9. What is the difference between attention and the feed-forward network?

    Perpendicular directions: attention along the token axis, the FFN along the feature axis.

  10. Our Transformer scored 0.750 where TF-IDF scored 0.780. The best explanation is…

    More epochs: 0.749. Smaller model: 0.750. Five times the data: 0.787.
